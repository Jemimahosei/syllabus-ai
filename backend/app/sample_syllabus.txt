Course Description
This course explores how data shapes the design of everyday information
systems. Students will learn to gather, analyze, and present data to inform
design decisions, working in small teams on a semester-long project with a
real community partner.

Learning Objectives
By the end of the course, students will be able to:
- Frame a design problem as a data question.
- Collect and clean small datasets responsibly.
- Present findings in clear, honest visualizations.

Required Materials

- A laptop capable of running a modern web browser and a spreadsheet application.

Artificial Intelligence Policy
Generative AI tools such as ChatGPT may be used for brainstorming and
outlining your ideas, and for getting unstuck when you are learning a new
tool. You must include a short disclosure note with any assignment where you
used an AI tool, describing what you used it for. You may NOT submit text,
code, or figures generated by an AI tool as your own work. Final submissions
must reflect your own thinking and your own words. Violations of this policy
will be treated as academic integrity violations and referred to the
university hearing board.

Late Work and Slip Days
Each student receives 3 slip days for the semester. You may use at most 2
slip days on any single assignment. Slip days extend the deadline by 24 hours
each and are used automatically, in order, with no penalty and no need to
email the instructor. After your slip days are used up, late work loses 10%
per day, and no assignment will be accepted more than 5 days after its
deadline. Quizzes and the midterm exam cannot use slip days, and there are
no makeups for missed quizzes except in documented emergencies.
